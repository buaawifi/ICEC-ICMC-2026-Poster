\begin{posterblock}{white}{smunavy}{smublue}{smulight}{Introduction}
\begingroup
\fontsize{24.0}{29.0}\selectfont
\setlength{\parskip}{1.0ex}
Large-scale liquid hydrogen (LH$_2$) cargo containment requires thin metallic barriers that remain gas-tight while accommodating thermal contraction and restrained in-plane deformation. In our previous work, an orthogonally corrugated 316L stainless-steel membrane was proposed and numerically optimized for LH$_2$ thermal-contraction and pressure-difference load cases, showing that corrugation can convert global contraction into local bending deformation and reduce strain demand in planar regions.

The present work experimentally validates this deformation-accommodation mechanism using Digital Image Correlation (DIC) under tensile loading, Acoustic Emission (AE) monitoring during cyclic testing, and liquid-nitrogen (LN$_2$) thermal cycling of a 0.2~m$^3$ demonstrator. Mechanical testing at LH$_2$ temperature is deferred because hydrogen-rated cryogenic testing requires dedicated safety infrastructure; room-temperature tests isolate the geometric mechanism, while LN$_2$ cycling provides a non-flammable cryogenic integrity screen.
